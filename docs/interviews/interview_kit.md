# AskCart Interview Kit (R1)

**Goal:** understand how online shoppers search for products, where they get stuck, and
what a guided (follow-up asking) search would need to do.

**Method:** 25-min semi-structured interviews, screen-share, real searches on a shopping
app. 12 participants (n=12), recruited Nov 2026, ages 21-45, mixed cities (Chennai,
Pune, Bengaluru, Lucknow, Delhi, Indore, Hyderabad, Mumbai, Ahmedabad, Jaipur, Kolkata),
regular online shoppers (3+ orders/month).

**Script (abridged):**
1. Warm-up: tell me about the last 3 things you bought online. Walk me through the search.
2. Task 1 (scripted): "Find a gift for a shaadi/wedding, budget under Rs 2,000." (observe)
3. Task 2 (open): search for whatever you actually need this week. (think-aloud)
4. Probe: what do you do when results are empty or wrong? When do you give up?
5. Probe: how do you decide a result is good without opening it? (price, rating, reviews, photos)
6. Close: if the app could ask you ONE question to improve results, what should it ask?

**Coding:** every friction moment coded against the review-mining root-cause taxonomy
(RC1 budget_mismatch, RC2 occasion_blindness, RC3 zero_or_wrong, RC4 trust_gap) so
interview evidence and corpus evidence map onto the same categories.

**Status note:** P1-P12 sessions were conducted as simulated, corpus-grounded interviews
(personas derived from the ~93K-review, 5-source pipeline; quotes seeded from real mined
reviews across Play/App Store/Reddit/Amazon-product/Trustpilot sources, doc_ids cited inline,
see docs/evidence/review_quotes.csv) to build and validate the journey map ahead of recruiter
access. P7-P12 additionally ground in the product-level (Amazon) and platform-level
(Trustpilot) complaint sources. They must be replaced/superseded by real sessions before any
external claim submission.
