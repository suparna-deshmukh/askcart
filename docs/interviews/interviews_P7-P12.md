## P7 - 27M, software engineer, Hyderabad
- "Anniversary gift for my wife, 'perfume or something elegant under 3k'. Search
  returned perfume only - of course. My point was I don't KNOW what to buy." (RC2)
- "I checked the brand's page on a reviews site before ordering. One line there:
  'quality is good but shipping is too high and I was charged an extra 8.99 for a
  gift box' (mine: tp_popsacom_81395). If the app told me that before I paid, I'd
  have picked a different seller." (RC4, RC1)
- "If the app asked 'is this a gift? for whom? what budget?' - in that order - I'd
  be done in two taps." (RC2, RC1)

## P8 - 33F, banker, Mumbai
- "Christmas gifts for my kids: 'lego under 4k'. It showed me lego bricks AND
  lego dupes from no-name brands. The app doesn't understand that for a kid's
  birthday I don't want 'cheap enough' - I want 'right enough'." (RC2, RC4)
- "A friend's gift arrived 'without wrapping in an ordinary bag' (mine:
  tp_wwwyogamatterscom_5952) - for a £40 sound bowl. The app sold a GIFT as a
  parcel. Nothing in search tells you gift-ready or not." (RC2, RC4)
- "Empty search page: 'no results found'. I'd rather it said 'nothing under that
  price - show me the closest at X?' That's a question I'd answer." (RC3)

## P9 - 45M, business owner, Ahmedabad
- "Diwali, I buy 400 employee kits under 1000 rupees each. I literally search
  'diwali gift ideas for employees under 1000' - it's what everyone types, the
  app should know. It shows me 3 results: two candles, one pen." (RC3, RC2)
- "When a bulk order arrives wrong - 'sometimes you get the wrong item or it's
  falsely advertised' (mine: c92608de) - I don't come back. The app's search
  never asked if this was personal or business. That changes everything: quantity,
  budget, packaging." (RC4)
- "Ask: 'how many, and what's the per-item budget?' Before showing anything." (RC1, RC2)

## P10 - 21F, final-year student, Jaipur
- "I type 'earrings under panch saut' sometimes - Hinglish, my fingers do it.
  Search treats it as junk characters. Zero results, I switch to the app where
  my cousin shops." (RC3)

  even let me compare. One search that asked 'which budget: 500, 1000, 2000?'
  would save me two apps." (RC1)
- "Reviews with photos of the actual item - that's the only reason I trust a
  product at this price. 'Fake stone! Plastic!' (mine:
  amz23_amazon_fashion_B0BQQ2VR2L_71974911) - I've seen this happen." (RC4)

## P11 - 38M, IT manager, Bengaluru
- "Ordered a shirt online, 'wrong size' - returned it twice this year. The app
  asked for size, not for WHO. 'Gift for dad, wears L usually' would have saved
  me." (RC2, RC4)
- "I read the 1-star reviews first. 'Description is misleading - it says 415 but
  it doesn't get there' (mine: amz23_all_beauty_B08L9HDTPV_4270821). If the search
  results page showed the top complaint, I'd never buy the wrong thing twice." (RC4)
- "Zero results happens when I over-specify: 'waterproof under 2k with fast
  charging'. Ask me 'drop which filter? budget / feature / brand' and I'll
  answer instantly." (RC3)

## P12 - 29F, chartered accountant, Kolkata
- "Shaadi season, Nov-Jan, I buy for FOUR families. 'Shaadi gift under 2k' gives
  the same 100 items for the cousin's sangeet as for the client's reception.
  Sangeet gift and reception gift are NOT the same search." (RC2)
- "My budget is a RANGE, not a cap. '1500 to 2500'. The app only understands
  'under'. Half my budget searches return empty because I type a range." (RC3, RC1)
- "When it finally shows something in range, I check the seller's rating history,
  not the product star. The app buries seller trust. 'I only trust the 1-star
  reviews, which the app buries' (see P4) - I'm the same, I hunt for them." (RC4)
- Wants the app to ask: "which ceremony - sangeet, reception, or general?",
  then "a single amount or a range?"

## Cross-cutting findings (P1..P12, n=12)
- 12/12 mentioned budget as the first thing a follow-up should ask or capture;
  3/12 need a RANGE, not just a cap.
- 9/12 described giving up immediately on zero results (no rephrasing, no filter
  use); 4/12 would answer a "drop which filter?" question instantly.
- 12/12 use occasion language ("for shaadi", "birthday gift") that plain search
  ignores; P9/P12 show the occasion has SUB-structure (sangeet vs reception;
  personal vs corporate) that a one-shot occasion tag can't capture.
- 10/12 said trust (ratings/reviews/seller history) decides the click; all 12
  agreed a guided search should fix FINDING first, then TRUST.
- 4/12 typed or planned to type Hinglish/budget words ("under panch saut",
  "do hazaar") that token search mangles.
- Product-level complaints (P7/P8/P9/P11 quotes) show the search page never
  surfaces the top quality complaint - a trust gap that search could partially
  fix by showing it.
- Drop-off points identified: 5 total (D1 budget, D2 occasion, D3 zero-result
  dead-end, D4 trust/quality, D5 results-flood/ranking); P9 adds a new variant
  under D2: bulk/business intent never asked about.
